# Human-Centric Copywriting & Forecourt Voice Rules

This rule documents the communication tone and copywriting standard observed and required across EasyFuel Pro (such as in `/site-survey`). All user-facing text, questionnaires, and checklists MUST adhere to these guidelines:

## 1. Empathetic, Field-Grounded Language
- Write for real human station operators, dealers, and forecourt technicians walking the pump islands, not abstract enterprise personas.
- Ground explanations in their daily reality: clipboards, pump nameplates, physical conduits, shift handovers, and KRA compliance audits.
- Example: *"Download our checklist, walk the forecourt with your station engineer to fill it out by hand, and upload the completed scan."*

## 2. Unambiguous Precision (Single Meaning Rule)
- Never ask vague questions that could mean either "current status" or "future desired behavior".
- Explicitly differentiate between:
  - **Baseline Today**: *"Current Forecourt Status: Are fuel sales transmitting to KRA in real-time today?"*
  - **Project Objective**: *"Project Goal: Do you require EasyFuel Pro to enable real-time automated KRA eTIMS transmission?"*
- Provide educational context BEFORE asking technical questions, never after.

## 3. Demystify Technical Terminology
- When technical terms appear (e.g., VSCU, OSCU, Pulser, PTS-2, ATG, RS-485 loop, Grounding), provide a one-sentence plain-language definition first.
- Explain *why* the parameter matters to the customer (e.g., why pulse output is necessary to link the dispenser computer to the controller; why redundant cellular connectivity prevents KRA downtime penalties).

## 4. Transparent & Low-Friction Tone
- Set honest, clear expectations up front (e.g., *"Fastest • 5 Minutes"*, *"Automatic on-device draft autosave"*).
- Avoid aggressive or hyperbolic sales jargon. Maintain a consultative, engineering-led posture: *"Direct engineering consultation with zero sales pressure"*, *"We'll help you design your forecourt integration"*.
- Welcome notes and additional observations in every section: allow the station manager to share non-standard site constraints in their own words.
